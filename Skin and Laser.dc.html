Why wavelength matters

Each colour of light stops at a different depth

A laser only treats what its wavelength reaches and what its target absorbs. This is the whole reason one machine cannot do everything, and why "laser" on a price list means almost nothing on its own.

Points to know

Laser on Asian skin is a different discipline

01

Rebound pigment is the real risk

Higher-melanin skin can darken in response to heat. Settings stay conservative and are raised only after we see how you healed.

02

A test patch, when it matters

For melasma or a first resurfacing, a small hidden area is treated first so the full pass is informed, not hopeful.

03

Aftercare is half the result

Sunscreen discipline and a pigment-suppressing routine matter as much as the device. You leave with both written down.

04

The doctor holds the handpiece

Settings are not handed to a machine operator. Every pass is decided and delivered by Dr. Yang.

Questions

Asked at almost every consultation

Ask Dr. Yang directly →
Will one session be enough?+

For individual dark spots, often yes. For melasma, redness or texture, no — and anyone promising it in one visit is either using settings that are too high or selling something else. Those respond to a course at safe energy.

Can I have this in summer?+

Yes, if you are disciplined about sun for the two weeks after. Treated skin pigments more readily, so a beach day soon afterwards can undo the session. If your trip includes one, we will treat at the end rather than the start.

How long am I visibly red?+

Toning, two to three hours. Vascular and pigment laser, one to two days. Fractional resurfacing, three days of redness and rough texture. Makeup is fine from the next morning in every case except resurfacing.

Does it thin the skin?+

The opposite, when it is done properly — controlled injury stimulates collagen, so skin measures slightly thicker over months. Thinning comes from over-treating, which is why intervals are respected.

Laser or a booster, for glow?+

A booster hydrates and plumps for weeks; a laser changes pigment and texture for months. Before an event, both in one visit is the usual answer — laser first, booster immediately after.
